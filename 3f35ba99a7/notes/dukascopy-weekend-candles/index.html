Lab notes · 11 October 2026

Two days a week that never happened

Download a year of EURUSD from Dukascopy's free feed and every weekend is there, minute by minute, as if the market had never closed. It did close. Those candles are made up — and nothing in a backtest will tell you so.

The weekend that wasn't

Dukascopy puts its whole price history on an open feed — no account, no key — so plenty of backtesting tools download from it. So do we. But when forex closes on Friday evening, the feed doesn't leave a gap. It goes on writing a one-minute candle for every minute, flat at the last price and with zero volume, until the market opens again on Sunday evening.

Here are two weeks of EURUSD one-minute candles, two and a half years apart, exactly as the feed sends them:

All that amber is fiction: the whole of Saturday, Sunday until the evening open, the tail of every Friday. Over a full week it's 48 hours — 2,880 of the week's 10,080 one-minute candles, 28.6%. Two days in every seven that never happened. (In the six days of March 2024 we managed to download, it came to 2,789 of 8,640: 32.3%.)

And in two and a half years, nothing has changed.

Why your backtest won't complain

Nothing breaks. That's the problem. Each made-up candle sits at a real price — the last one traded — so it looks perfectly reasonable. But a backtest running over them is trading a closed market: the average true range sinks toward zero every weekend, moving averages go flat, volatility filters switch off, and anything that counts candles — exit after 24 bars, the high of the last 50 — quietly counts two days that weren't there.

How to catch them

Filtering on price won't help: the price is fine. The only field that tells the truth is the volume, and you have to read it candle by candle, not day by day — Sunday is half closed and half the week's opening.

One catch, though: not every zero-volume candle is fiction. On weekdays a handful of minutes come back flat with no volume — quiet minutes, nobody trading — and every so often a candle that does move arrives with zero volume too. Throw that one away and you've made a hole in the history that wasn't there either.

So when BotFactoryFX downloads from Dukascopy, it drops a zero-volume candle only when it's flat or falls in the forex weekend, and keeps a weekday candle that moves. If you get Dukascopy data some other way, have a look at your weekends before you trust a backtest built on them.

And sometimes, nothing at all

One more quirk: now and then the feed simply doesn't send a file. In each of those two weeks, one day never arrived, however many times we asked — Friday 1 March 2024 and Saturday 5 September 2026. That's the striped row.
