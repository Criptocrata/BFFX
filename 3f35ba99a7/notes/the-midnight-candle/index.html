The midnight candle that knew the rest of the day

It started with a strategy that was too good: it traded at midnight and almost never lost. The strategy was fine. The history wasn't.

Too good to be true

On 29 September 2026 we were judging strategies on a broker's EURUSD H1 history, downloaded through MetaTrader 5, and one of them looked wonderful: a short taken when two Ichimoku lines, Tenkan and Kijun, were falling. From 2002 to 2016 it made 773 of its 961 trades at midnight, won 89% of them and finished +$30,977. Too good. So we went and looked at the candles.

A candle that already knows

Here's a perfectly ordinary day from that history, 13 January 2010:

Look at the first candle. The one stamped 00:00 has a 94-pip wick up to 1.4579 — and the rest of the day doesn't get there until 13:00, when it tops out at 1.45785, half a pip short. At midnight, that candle already “knew” the high of one o'clock in the afternoon.

And it wasn't one day. It happened on most trading days, year after year, until 2017 — when it simply stopped:

In numbers: from 2002 to 2017, the midnight candle had a wick of more than 30 pips on 9 days out of 10, and in 94.0% of them the tip of that wick sat within 3 pips of the high or low of the rest of the day. On the previous day's high or low? Just 2.6%. If those wicks were noise, both figures would be about the same. From 2018 on: not once.

Why a search falls for it

A strategy that enters at midnight and aims at the day's extreme is reading the answer before the question — and a genetic search, trying thousands of rule sets, finds that shortcut in no time. With the midnight wicks trimmed, our too-good strategy's +$30,977 shrank to +$239.

Why the usual tests don't see it

Most robustness tests shuffle, resample or cut whole candles: Monte Carlo, out-of-sample splits, random-direction benchmarks. The leak lives inside each candle, so it sails through all of them. Our judge only caught the strategy because the stretch it keeps aside — 2017 onwards — was already clean. Had the whole history been leaky, it would have passed.

Check yours

Compare each candle's high and low with those of the next 24 hours. If the candles of one particular hour keep matching the coming day's extremes, that hour is carrying the future. By the way, the prices come from the broker, not from MetaTrader, which passes on whatever history it's given. We're not naming the broker: what matters is that it can happen to any history — and that you can check.

How we measured

EURUSD H1, 172,281 candles from 30 December 1998 to 28 September 2026, as downloaded on 29 September 2026, measured again on 11 October 2026. The wick is the part of the candle beyond its body (open and close); a tip matches an extreme when it's within 3 pips of it. The program's own detector flags the 00:00 candle on 3,407 of 3,984 days, from 15 January 2002 to 28 April 2017. The search figures are from our run of 29 September 2026.
